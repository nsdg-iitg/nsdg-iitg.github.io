Jawaharlal fabricates electrical devices on single metal-oxide nanowires and modifies their surfaces for sensing applications. He is co-supervised by Prof. D. Pamu.

CuO nanowires (80–300 nm in diameter, 10–40 µm in length) are grown by thermal oxidation of copper foil. Individual wires are isolated, and 2-terminal, 4-terminal and transfer-length-method (TLM) structures are fabricated on them by laser and electron-beam lithography.

A short (7-minute) RF nitrogen plasma treatment increased the four-probe conductivity of single wires from 8–11 S/m to 40–105 S/m and reduced the turn-on voltage of 2-terminal devices from 1.75 V to 0.55 V. The increase scales with the surface-to-volume ratio, which indicates that nitrogen dopes only the surface region. Longer exposure roughens the surface and partially reduces CuO to Cu2O, so that the conductance decreases again. TEM, single-wire Raman spectroscopy and EDX support this interpretation.
