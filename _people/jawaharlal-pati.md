Jawaharlal makes electrical devices on single metal-oxide nanowires and tunes their surfaces for sensing. He is co-supervised by Prof. D. Pamu.

He grows CuO nanowires (80–300 nm in diameter, 10–40 µm long) by thermal oxidation of copper foil. He picks out single wires and makes 2-terminal, 4-terminal and transfer-length-method (TLM) structures on them by laser and electron-beam lithography.

A short (7 minute) RF nitrogen plasma raised the four-probe conductivity of single wires from 8–11 S/m to 40–105 S/m, and lowered the turn-on voltage of 2-terminal devices from 1.75 V to 0.55 V. The gain grows with the surface-to-volume ratio, which shows the nitrogen dopes only the surface. Longer exposure roughens the surface and partly reduces CuO to Cu2O, so conductance falls again. TEM, single-wire Raman and EDX confirm this picture.
