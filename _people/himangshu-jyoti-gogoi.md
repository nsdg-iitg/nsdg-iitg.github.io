Himangshu built the group's work on hybrid perovskite memristors, from the first devices to switching physics, measurement methods, circuit models and defect spectroscopy.

**Two switching modes in one cell.** In ITO/PEDOT:PSS/MAPbI3/PCBM/Ag memristors, he found that the compliance current picks between two bipolar switching modes of opposite polarity.
- *Type A* (1 mA compliance) is a field-driven redox mode of the iodide/iodine-vacancy filament. It sets at 0.47 V, resets gradually at −0.32 V, and gives multilevel states.
- *Type B* (0.5 mA compliance) resets abruptly by Joule heating, with an ON/OFF ratio near 5,900.

Run in one sweep, the two modes give complementary resistive switching in a single cell, with a 0.7 V read window. This can suppress sneak currents in crossbar arrays without a second, anti-serial cell. It was the first report of this in a hybrid perovskite memristor. He also traced the conduction in each state: tunnelling in the high-resistance state of Type A, diffusion current in Type B, and Ohmic conduction through the filament in the low-resistance state.

**How the measurement changes the device.** He showed that a perovskite memristor's figures of merit depend strongly on how it is measured.
- A faster voltage sweep raises the reset voltage and current.
- A higher compliance current builds a stronger filament and raises the ON/OFF ratio from 17 to 225.
- A larger device area lowers the forming voltage from 1.55 V to 0.86 V and lowers the high-resistance state, while the low-resistance state is set by the filament alone.

From this he proposed a step-by-step test protocol for perovskite memristors.

**Forming and circuit models.** On Ge2Se3 chalcogenide memristors, he compared five ways of electroforming a cell, and found that a positive voltage sweep gives the most repeatable switching afterwards. He fitted his perovskite devices with the Yakopcic SPICE model to within about 7%, so they can be used in circuit simulation.

**Defects behind switching.** He made flexible perovskite memristors on PET and studied them with temperature-dependent DLTS. This found methylammonium vacancies with an activation energy of 0.48 eV, low enough to help the filament form.

**Flexible, lead-free devices.** He co-developed the group's flexible, air-stable Cs3Bi2I9 memristors and their use as artificial synapses, and contributed to the study of ion migration in perovskite LEDs.
