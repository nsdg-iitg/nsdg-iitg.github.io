Himangshu established the group's work on hybrid perovskite memristors.

**Two switching modes in one cell.** In ITO/PEDOT:PSS/MAPbI3/PCBM/Ag memristors, the compliance current chooses between two bipolar switching modes of opposite polarity. One is a field-driven redox mode of the iodide/vacancy filament. The other is a Joule-heating mode. Using both in one sweep gives complementary resistive switching in a single cell, which can suppress sneak currents in crossbar arrays without a second, anti-serial cell.

**How to measure a memristor.** He showed how the scan rate, compliance current, pulse settings and device area change the switching voltages and ON/OFF ratio, and proposed a standard test protocol. He also compared forming methods for chalcogenide memristors, and fitted his devices with SPICE (Yakopcic) models.

**Defects behind switching.** With Basit, he used DLTS to find methylammonium vacancies in flexible perovskite memristors with an activation energy of 0.48 eV, low enough to help the filament form.

He also wrote the group's review of flexible perovskite memristors, and built the group's first website.
