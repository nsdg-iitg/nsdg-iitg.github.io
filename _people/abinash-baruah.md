Abinash fabricated green MAPbBr3 perovskite light-emitting diodes (PeLEDs) and investigated the mechanisms responsible for their luminance degradation. He defended his PhD thesis in November 2026.

The devices use an ITO/PEDOT:PSS/MAPbBr3/TPBi/LiF/Al stack, fabricated by solution processing with anti-solvent treatment. They reach a luminance of about 13,000 cd/m², with an emission peak at 533 nm and a linewidth of 20 nm.

To identify the factors that limit the lifetime, he combined temperature-dependent impedance spectroscopy (77–380 K), electric modulus analysis, deep-level transient spectroscopy (DLTS) and cross-sectional EDX. The main findings are as follows.

- **Bromide-ion migration as the dominant degradation mechanism.** Under bias, Br⁻ ions drift and accumulate at the anode, which gives rise to a very large apparent permittivity at low frequency. The ion diffusion coefficient, mobility and density were extracted. During rest, the ions partially redistribute, and cycled operation approximately doubles the T50 lifetime.
- **Effect of grain size.** Small grains (~62 nm) store more charge at grain boundaries, which lowers the injection barrier and results in a lower turn-on voltage and higher luminance, but also in faster ion redistribution and a shorter lifetime. Large grains (~150 nm) give lower luminance and a longer lifetime.
- **Transport and traps.** Conduction is consistent with correlated barrier hopping, and DLTS reveals a shallow trap at 0.30 eV, attributed to bromide-vacancy-related ionic defects.
