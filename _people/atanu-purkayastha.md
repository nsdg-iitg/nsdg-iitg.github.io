Atanu worked on solar cells made from lead-free tin perovskites. He combined device fabrication with optical and electrical simulation, and checked the environmental cost of each design with a full life-cycle assessment.

**Making tin cells better.** Adding 15% phenylethylammonium iodide (PEAI) to FASnI3 forms a mixed 2D/3D film with larger grains and fewer defects. This raised efficiency from 6.41% to 11.03%. The 2D/3D cells also lost only 25% in 50 hours, against more than 75% in 16 hours for plain 3D cells. An 80 nm MgF2 anti-reflection layer brought them to 12.6%.

**Designing tandem and bifacial cells.** He wrote transfer-matrix algorithms to optimise the layer thicknesses of tandem and triple-junction cells (protected by software copyright SW-17416/2023). With them, he found ITO to be the best interlayer for a CsFAPbX3/FASnI3 tandem. His lead-free MAGeI3/2D-3D FASnI3 tandem design reaches a simulated 31.34%. A bifacial version of the tin cell gains up to 39% more power over snow.

**Light trapping.** In simulation, 10 nm silver nanoparticles at the PCBM/Ag interface raise absorption by about 8%. In the hole transport layer, the same particles hurt.

**Sustainability.** His life-cycle assessments show tin perovskite modules paying back their manufacturing energy in 0.53–0.57 years, at 0.03–0.07 kg CO2 per kWh. That is well below lead perovskite and crystalline silicon modules in India.
