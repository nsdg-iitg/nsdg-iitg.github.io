Atanu worked on lead-free tin perovskite solar cells. He combined device fabrication with optical and electrical simulation, and evaluated the environmental impact of each design through life-cycle assessment.

**Improved tin perovskite cells.** The addition of 15% phenylethylammonium iodide (PEAI) to FASnI3 forms a mixed 2D/3D film with larger grains and a lower defect density. This increased the efficiency from 6.41% to 11.03%. The 2D/3D cells retained 75% of their initial efficiency after 50 hours, compared with a loss of more than 75% within 16 hours for 3D cells. An 80 nm MgF2 anti-reflection coating further increased the efficiency to 12.6%.

**Design of tandem and bifacial cells.** He developed transfer-matrix algorithms to optimise the layer thicknesses of tandem and triple-junction cells (software copyright SW-17416/2023). Using these, he identified ITO as the most suitable interlayer for a CsFAPbX3/FASnI3 tandem. His lead-free MAGeI3/2D-3D FASnI3 tandem design reaches a simulated efficiency of 31.34%. In simulation, a bifacial version of the tin cell gains up to 39% in output power over a snow surface.

**Light trapping.** In simulation, 10 nm silver nanoparticles at the PCBM/Ag interface increase absorption by about 8%, whereas the same particles in the hole transport layer reduce performance.

**Sustainability.** His life-cycle assessments estimate an energy payback time of 0.53–0.57 years and emissions of 0.03–0.07 kg CO2 per kWh for tin perovskite modules. Both values are well below those estimated for lead perovskite and crystalline silicon modules in India.
