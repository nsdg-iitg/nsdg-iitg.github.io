Radharani develops resistive-switching memories (memristors) based on all-inorganic, lead-free perovskites and integrates them into crossbar arrays on flexible substrates. She is co-supervised by Prof. Roy P. Paily, Dept. of EEE.

The devices use Cs3Bi2I9, a bismuth-based perovskite, in a PET/ITO/PEDOT:PSS/Cs3Bi2I9/PCBM/Ag stack. In contrast to most perovskite memristors, the switching layer is deposited by electron-beam evaporation of a hot-pressed pellet, a vacuum process that is scalable to large areas.

The devices show bipolar switching at low voltages (around ±0.3 V) with an ON/OFF ratio above 10⁵. They remain functional after 30 days at more than 80% relative humidity without encapsulation, and under bending to a 5 mm radius. Conductive AFM indicates that the conducting filament is formed by intrinsic iodine vacancies in the film rather than by silver from the electrode.

She has fabricated a flexible 4×4 crossbar array with 81% device yield and demonstrated synaptic behaviour, including paired-pulse facilitation and long-term potentiation and depression over 120 pulses, at about 0.1 µJ per event. Her current work addresses larger crossbar arrays based on all-inorganic perovskites.
