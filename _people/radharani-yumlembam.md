Radharani builds resistive-switching memories (memristors) from all-inorganic, lead-free perovskites, and puts them into crossbar arrays on flexible plastic. She is co-supervised by Prof. Roy P. Paily, Dept. of EEE.

Her devices use Cs3Bi2I9, a bismuth-based perovskite, in a PET/ITO/PEDOT:PSS/Cs3Bi2I9/PCBM/Ag stack. Unlike most perovskite memristors, the switching layer is deposited by electron-beam evaporation of a hot-pressed pellet, a vacuum process that scales to large areas.

The devices show bipolar switching at low voltages (around ±0.3 V) with an ON/OFF ratio above 10⁵. They keep working after 30 days in more than 80% relative humidity without any encapsulation, and when bent to a 5 mm radius. Conductive AFM shows that the conducting filament forms from the film's own iodine vacancies, not from the silver electrode.

She has built a flexible 4×4 crossbar with 81% yield, and shown that the cells behave like biological synapses: paired-pulse facilitation, and long-term potentiation and depression over 120 pulses, at about 0.1 µJ per event. Her current work is on larger crossbar arrays with all-inorganic perovskites.
