A nanowire has a very large surface for its volume, so small changes at its surface change its current strongly. That makes single nanowires sensitive sensors, provided we can make reliable contacts to them and control their surface.

### What we have shown

- **Single-wire devices.** CuO nanowires grown by thermal oxidation of copper are isolated one at a time. 2-terminal, 4-terminal and TLM structures are patterned on them by laser and e-beam lithography.
- **Plasma surface doping.** A 7-minute RF nitrogen plasma raised single-wire conductivity about 10×, from 8–11 S/m to 40–105 S/m. Thinner wires gained more, which shows the doping is at the surface only. Longer plasma damages the surface and lowers conductance again.

Work on nanodevices that combine energy generation and storage is under way.
