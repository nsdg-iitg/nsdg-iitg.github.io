{{ site.group_name }} · IIT Guwahati

Devices that harvest energy, emit light and remember

The group fabricates and models semiconductor devices based on halide perovskites, organic semiconductors and nanowires. The work extends from thin-film growth and device fabrication to defect spectroscopy, device simulation and circuit design.

Current devices include flexible perovskite memristors and crossbar arrays for neuromorphic computing, lead-free and tandem perovskite solar cells, green perovskite light-emitting diodes, and sensors based on single nanowires. More recent work addresses organic light-emitting diodes, a memristor-based artificial olfactory system, and energy-harvesting devices and circuits with supercapacitor storage. The group has also begun work on semiconductor packaging.

The aim is devices for real applications: memories and artificial synapses for low-power neuromorphic computing and wearable electronics, solar cells for outdoor, indoor and bifacial use, efficient light sources for displays, and sensors for health and environmental monitoring. The experimental work is supported by industry-standard TCAD and by CEISIM, a drift–diffusion simulator developed in the group for coupled electronic and ionic transport, which form the basis for analytical and compact models.
