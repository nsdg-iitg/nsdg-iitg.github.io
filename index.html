Chapter I

One material family, three behaviours.

  1. Equilibrium

    In the dark and at zero bias the Fermi level is flat. The built-in field tilts the bands of the perovskite layer.

  2. Energy harvesting

    Light generates electrons and holes. The field sweeps them to opposite contacts, the quasi-Fermi levels split and a photovoltage appears.

  3. Light emission

    Forward bias beyond flat band injects electrons and holes from the contacts. They recombine in the perovskite and photons come out.

  4. Memory

    The same lattice holds mobile ions and vacancies. A voltage sweep forms a conducting filament and ruptures it again, so the resistance depends on history.

Chapter II

From thin-film growth to compact models.

The group fabricates and models semiconductor devices based on halide perovskites, organic semiconductors and nanowires. The work extends from thin-film growth and device fabrication to defect spectroscopy, device simulation and circuit design.

Current devices include flexible perovskite memristors and crossbar arrays for neuromorphic computing, lead-free and tandem perovskite solar cells, green perovskite light-emitting diodes, and sensors based on single nanowires. More recent work addresses organic light-emitting diodes, a memristor-based artificial olfactory system, and energy-harvesting devices and circuits with supercapacitor storage. The group has also begun work on semiconductor packaging.

For each device, we study the underlying physics, namely carrier generation and recombination, electronic and ionic transport, and the energy and spatial distribution of traps and mobile ions. The experimental methods include deep-level transient spectroscopy, impedance spectroscopy, low-temperature I–V and C–V measurements and nanoscale imaging. These are supported by TCAD and by CEISIM, a drift–diffusion simulator developed in the group, which form the basis for analytical and compact models.
