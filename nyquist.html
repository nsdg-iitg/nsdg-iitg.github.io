Methods and assumptions
  • Mott–Schottky. One-sided abrupt junction with uniform doping in the depletion approximation: 1/C² = 2(Vbi − V − kBT/q)/(q ε N), with C per unit area. The doping density follows from the slope and Vbi from the intercept. The apparent doping profile uses the local slope, N(W) = 2/(q ε |d(1/C²)/dV|) at W = ε/C.
  • Circuit fit. Complex non-linear least squares on Z′ and Z″ together (Levenberg–Marquardt), started from several automatic guesses. Standard errors come from the covariance matrix of the fit. A constant-phase element has Z = 1/(Q (jω)n), and the Warburg element Z = σ(1 − j)/√ω. A good fit does not prove that the circuit is the correct physical model; different circuits can fit the same spectrum.
  • Trap density from C–f. Method of Walter et al., J. Appl. Phys. 80, 4411 (1996), for a junction with a depletion width W and built-in voltage Vbi. Only traps that can follow the a.c. signal contribute, so the energy axis is the demarcation energy Eω = kBT ln(2πν₀/ω).
